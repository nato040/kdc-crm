# Campaign Brief: "Get More Out of It" — 3-Week Customer Deep-Engagement Sprint

## 1. Campaign Overview

**Campaign name:** Get More Out of It
**Summary:** A 3-week, email + SMS sprint that nudges existing DTC customers to rediscover products they already love, explore complementary items, and level up from one-time buyers to engaged repeat customers — using only owned channels and zero paid media.
**Primary objective:** Increase repeat-purchase rate and order frequency among existing customers during the 3-week window.
**Specific goal (pick the one that matches your baseline):** Lift repeat-purchase rate by 15% and average order frequency by 10% among customers who receive at least one campaign touch, vs. the prior 3-week period.
**Secondary objectives:** grow SMS opt-ins among email-only subscribers; lift average order value (AOV) via cross-sell; surface post-purchase UGC and reviews to feed future content.

## 2. Target Audience

**Primary segment — "Engaged but Under-Indexed" customers.** Existing customers who have purchased at least once in the last 12 months, are still opted in to email, and have not bought in the last 45-90 days. They know the brand, like it enough to stay subscribed, but aren't in an active buying rhythm.

**Secondary segment — "First-time buyers past the 30-day mark."** Customers whose first order has had time to arrive, be used, and form an opinion — prime candidates for a well-timed second purchase.

**Pain points and motivations:** they want products that fit their life without decision fatigue, value small delights and personalization, and respond to signals that the brand remembers them. They're skeptical of generic "we miss you" messages and tune out discount-only emails.

**Where they spend time:** inbox, text messages, and Instagram/TikTok feeds. The campaign lives in email and SMS; organic social can amplify but isn't required.

**Buying stage:** post-purchase consideration — they're not being introduced to the brand, they're being reminded why they liked it and shown what to try next.

## 3. Key Messages

**Core message:** "There's more here for you — and we picked it with you in mind."

**Supporting messages:**

- *Personal relevance.* "Based on what you loved last time, here's what fits next." Proof: product pairings, bestseller-by-category logic, customer reviews from similar buyers.
- *Low-friction rediscovery.* "Restock the things you already know work." Proof: replenishment timing based on typical use cycles; one-tap reorder.
- *Small upgrade, big difference.* "Try the next step up — many of our customers do after their first order." Proof: review snippets, before/after use cases, bundle pricing logic (even without a discount).
- *You belong here.* "You're part of [brand] — here's something just for customers." Proof: early access, limited-run drop, or content that isn't on the public site.

**Tone:** warm, specific, confident. Not apologetic ("we miss you"), not discount-forward. Treat the customer as someone who already made a smart choice.

**Message variation by channel:**

- *Email:* full story, imagery, 2-3 product recommendations per send, clear primary CTA.
- *SMS:* one idea, one link, one CTA. Max ~160 characters. Human tone, no emojis unless brand voice allows.

## 4. Channel Strategy

Owned channels only. Paid and most earned channels are out of scope for this sprint given the minimal-budget constraint.

**Email (primary — highest-volume touchpoint)**

- *Why:* established list, zero incremental cost, best channel for narrative + multiple product showcases.
- *Formats:* hero + grid product emails, editorial-style "pairs well with" emails, plain-text founder note for segment 2.
- *Effort:* medium — 5-6 sends across 3 weeks, mostly templated.

**SMS (secondary — precision nudges)**

- *Why:* highest open rate of any owned channel, ideal for time-sensitive nudges and quick "you haven't seen this yet" drops.
- *Formats:* short text with deep link to a curated PDP or collection, one nudge per week max to avoid opt-outs.
- *Effort:* low — 3 sends across 3 weeks.

**Organic social and on-site merchandising (supporting, optional)**

- *Why:* reinforce the campaign narrative for customers who check Instagram stories or return to the site from an email.
- *Formats:* 3-5 stories or posts echoing the weekly theme; homepage hero module matching the active week.
- *Effort:* low if you're already posting — just align existing cadence to campaign themes.

**Budget note:** No paid media allocated. If any budget becomes available mid-campaign, the highest-leverage use is a small Meta retargeting layer against engaged email openers to reinforce SMS and email CTAs.

## 6. Content Pieces Needed

**Must-have**

- 6 email designs or templates (4 designed, 2 can be plain text)
- 3 SMS message variants (with fallback copy for non-SMS opt-ins routed to email)
- Dynamic product block / segment logic in ESP (primary segment, past-category segment, first-time-buyer segment)
- 1 "just for customers" asset for Week 2 (bundle page, early-access landing, or editorial content)
- Segmentation rules documented (criteria for primary vs. secondary segment, suppression for recent purchasers)

**Nice-to-have**

- 3-5 organic social posts/stories aligned to weekly themes
- Homepage hero module updated weekly to match campaign
- SMS opt-in CTA inserted into Week 1 email footers to grow SMS list
- Post-purchase flow updated so anyone buying during the sprint gets a thank-you note that references the campaign theme

**Production timeline:** design and copy lock for Weeks 1-2 by end of the week before launch; Week 3 assets can lock midway through Week 2 if needed.

## 7. Success Metrics

**Primary KPI:** repeat-purchase rate among the campaigned audience vs. an equivalent control period (prior 3 weeks). Target: +15%.

**Secondary KPIs:**

- Revenue per recipient across the 6 email + 3 SMS sends (track against last comparable 3-week period)
- Email open rate (target: >35% for engaged segment), click rate (target: >3%)
- SMS click-through rate (target: >10%) and opt-out rate (keep <1% per send)
- AOV for campaign-attributed orders vs. non-campaign baseline (target: flat or up)
- New SMS opt-ins from email-only subscribers during the sprint

**Tracking:** UTM every link (source = email or sms, campaign = get-more-out-of-it, content = send-name), use your ESP's revenue attribution, and pull a weekly snapshot.

**Reporting cadence:** quick read-out at end of each week (what's working, what to adjust mid-flight), full recap within 5 business days of campaign close.

## 8. Budget Allocation

No paid media budget. Costs are time and tooling:

- ~2-3 days of copy and design across the 3 weeks
- ~1 day of ESP/SMS-platform setup (segments, dynamic blocks, automations)
- Incremental SMS send costs (typically fractions of a cent per message — confirm with your provider)
- Optional: small product cost if Week 2's "just for customers" moment includes a gift-with-purchase or exclusive bundle

If any discretionary spend opens up, the highest-leverage use is (1) a photographer/UGC budget for Week 2's hero asset or (2) retargeting email-openers on Meta with a ~$2K-$5K test layer.

## 9. Risks and Mitigations

- **Risk: list fatigue and opt-outs.** Six emails + three SMS in three weeks is on the high end. *Mitigation:* tight segmentation (suppress anyone who purchased in the prior 14 days), strong subject lines, and cap any given customer at one touch per channel per day.
- **Risk: messages read as generic "we miss you" discount bait.** *Mitigation:* lead with personalization and utility ("based on what you bought"), not discounts. Reserve any discount for a single Week 3 SMS if performance is soft.
- **Risk: Week 2 "just for customers" asset slips.** That one email is the campaign's emotional peak. *Mitigation:* lock the concept in pre-launch and have a backup (a curated editorial collection from existing inventory) that requires no new production.
- **Risk: attribution noise — hard to isolate campaign impact without a control.** *Mitigation:* create a small hold-out group (5-10% of the target segment) who receives no campaign messages, and compare repeat-purchase rate post-campaign.
